About Hawassa

History of Hawassa

Hawassa city was established in 1952 during the period of Emperor Hailesilasie. The city got its name from Lake Hawassa, which means "Wide" in the Sidama language.

Hawassa city is located in the Sidama National Regional State on the shores of Lake Hawassa in the Great Rift Valley, 275 km south of Addis Ababa and 1,125 km north of Nairobi, Kenya. The city lies on the Trans African Highway-4: an international road that stretches from Cairo (Egypt) to Cape Town (South Africa).

Geographically, the city lies between 6°55'0'' to 7°06'0'' latitude North and 38°25'0'' to 38°34'0'' longitude East.

Hawassa city is bounded by Lake Hawassa in the West, Oromia Region in the North, Wendogenet woreda in the East, and Shebedino woreda in the South. The city administration has an area of 157.2 sq.km, divided into 8 sub-cities and 32 Kebeles. These eight sub-cities are Hayek Dare, Menehariya, Tabor, Misrak, Bahile Adarash, Addis Ketema, Hawela-Tula, and Mehal Ketema. Currently, the city serves as the seat of Sidama Region & Hawassa City Administration.

Culture & People

Hawassa is a vibrant and welcoming city known for its cultural diversity and peaceful way of life. As the capital of the Sidama Region, the city is home to the Sidama people along with many other ethnic groups such as Amhara, Oromo, Wolayta, Gurage, Kembata, and Hadya. This mix of communities creates a rich blend of traditions, languages, and lifestyles.

The city's main languages are Sidamu Afoo and Amharic, while English is commonly spoken among students and young people. Hawassa is famous for its hospitality—visitors often feel at home because of the polite and friendly nature of the people.

The Sidama people celebrate Fichee-Chambalaalla, the Sidama New Year, which is officially recognized by UNESCO.

Traditional foods such as enset (wesse), chechebsa, and fresh tilapia fish from Lake Hawassa reflect the region's unique culinary identity. The coffee ceremony, known locally as Buna Qalaa, is a symbol of respect and togetherness.

Hawassa's religious life is also diverse. Orthodox Christians, Protestants, Muslims, and Catholics all live peacefully within the city, contributing to its reputation as a harmonious and inclusive place.

The city is rich in arts and music. Traditional dances and instruments—like the feresha and kebero—are often showcased during cultural festivals and ceremonies. At the same time, Hawassa has a modern urban lifestyle with lakeside resorts, cafés, sports activities, and a lively youth culture.

Overall, the people of Hawassa are known for their unity, respect, and strong community values, making the city not only beautiful but culturally meaningful and welcoming to everyone who visits.

Climate & Location

Hawassa is the capital of the Sidama Region in southern Ethiopia, located 275 km south of Addis Ababa along the Addis–Hawassa highway. The city sits on the eastern shore of Lake Hawassa, one of the most beautiful lakes in the Great Rift Valley. It lies at an elevation of 1,708 meters above sea level, with coordinates 7.05° N and 38.47° E.

Known for its scenic views, abundant birdlife, and resorts, Lake Hawassa is a key feature that makes the city a popular destination. Hawassa's strategic location has established it as a center for tourism, conferences, business, and regional administration.

The city enjoys a pleasant tropical highland climate, making it comfortable year-round for residents and visitors. Average daytime temperatures range from 20°C to 28°C, with cooler nights due to the city's elevation. Hawassa experiences two main rainy seasons: short rains from March to May and long rains from June to September, with a dry season from October to February. The climate is characterized by moderate humidity influenced by Lake Hawassa, plenty of sunshine, clean air, and a generally mild atmosphere.

City Importance

Hawassa, the capital of Ethiopia's Sidama Region, is a vital city in southern Ethiopia known for its economic, cultural, and strategic importance. As a commercial and industrial hub, it hosts active markets, the Hawassa Industrial Park, and tourism along Lake Hawassa.

Strategically, it serves as the regional administrative center and a transport link between southern Ethiopia and the capital. The city is also an educational and research center, home to Hawassa University and various research institutions.

Culturally, it is diverse, celebrating local traditions and festivals that enrich the social life. With its scenic lake, parks, and favorable climate for agriculture, Hawassa is a center for eco-tourism, recreation, and sustainable development in the region.
